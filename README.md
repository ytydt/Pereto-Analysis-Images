Figure S1 (pareto_cost_accuracy.pdf): Cost–accuracy Pareto frontier on College Math Pro. Each point represents a multi-agent reasoning system; filled circles (●) denote standard configurations, open upward triangles (△) denote compute-matched (scaled) variants, the diamond (◆) denotes DD-lite (adaptive gating), and the star (★) denotes full DD. The x-axis shows per-question token consumption (tok/q) on a log scale; the y-axis shows accuracy. The dashed red line traces the Pareto frontier. DD-lite (24,815 tok/q, 0.838 ± 0.003) and CoT (3,748 tok/q, 0.790) jointly define the Pareto frontier — no method achieves higher accuracy at any cost up to 307K tok/q. DD (40,570 tok/q) matches DT (40,262 tok/q) in cost but leads by +9.6 pp in accuracy. Annotation arrows highlight two findings: (1) scaling SoM from 3 to 8 agents (5× token budget, 115K tok/q) yields zero accuracy gain (0.797 → 0.797); (2) DD-lite achieves the same accuracy as full DD while consuming 39% fewer tokens. The light-shaded region below CoT accuracy (0.790) marks methods that degrade below single-agent Chain-of-Thought — four of six baselines (CFMAD, DT, and their scaled variants) fall in this region, representing negative return on compute investment. DD-lite is 6× more token-efficient than SoM per percentage point gained over CoT (5,170 vs 30,993 tok/pp). Prompt and business logic optimization reduced DD from ~90K (original submission) to 40,570 tok/q with no accuracy loss (Welch's t test p = 0.61, n.s.).

Figure S2 (pareto_three_datasets.pdf): Cost–accuracy comparison across three datasets. Three panels compare DD and compute-matched baselines on Math Pro (left, mathematics), College Math (center, mathematics), and MedxpertQA-R (right, medicine). Stars (★) denote DD, diamonds (◆) denote DD-lite, filled circles (●) denote standard-budget methods, and open triangles (△) denote compute-matched (scaled) variants. Error bars indicate ±1 SD across 3 independent runs. DD achieves the highest accuracy on all three datasets: 0.843 ± 0.003 on Math Pro, 0.933 ± 0.005 on College Math, and 0.183 ± 0.009 on MedxpertQA-R. The Math Pro panel additionally includes DD-lite, CoT, and SoM for complete cost–accuracy context. Compute-matched baselines receive 2.8–7.6× DD's token budget yet consistently underperform: SoM-8 (115K tok/q) gains zero accuracy over SoM on Math Pro (0.797 → 0.797); DT-×5 (131K tok/q) remains below CoT on Math Pro (0.761 < 0.790); CFMAD-3v (307K tok/q) trails DD by 12.6 pp on Math Pro, 15.3 pp on College Math, and 6.0 pp on MedxpertQA-R. The cross-domain consistency — spanning a 65 pp base-accuracy difference between math (84%) and medicine (19%) — confirms that the advantage is architectural rather than budget-driven: scaling tokens within existing debate paradigms cannot close the performance gap with DD.

Figure S3 (lightweight_dd_pareto.pdf): Pareto frontier of DD-lite gating strategies. Each point represents a candidate gating rule derived from three decomposition-diagnostic features: subtask count k, solver confidence c, and hedge word frequency h. The x-axis shows the skip rate (fraction of questions where debate is bypassed); the y-axis shows ΔAcc relative to full DD. On Math Pro, 13 single-feature rules and 6 conjunction/union combinations were evaluated; on MedxpertQA-R, 10 single rules and 5 union combinations. On Math Pro, 5 distinct threshold settings all achieve ΔAcc = 0 at varying skip rates — the gate is insensitive to the exact confidence cutoff (0.99 vs 1.0) and to the choice of hard-end rule. The selected DD-lite configuration (union of the simple-and-confident gate k ≤ 4 ∧ c ≥ 0.99 with the uncertain-extreme gate h ≥ 3 or k > 5 ∧ (h > 0 ∨ c < 0.99)) achieves the maximum skip rate of 49% (36/74 questions) with ΔAcc = 0, corresponding to a 38.8% token saving (40,570 → 24,815 tok/q). On MedxpertQA-R, 7 of 15 candidate rules achieve ΔAcc ≥ 0; the optimal union of c ≤ 0.8 ∧ h ≥ 3 with k ≥ 5 ∧ h ≥ 2 skips 21–29% of questions while improving accuracy by +1–2 pp, as it captures genuinely harmful debate cases (R/H < 1.0 in the targeted feature intervals). The two gate classes are structurally motivated: (1) simple-and-confident problems (k ≤ 4 ∧ c ≥ 0.99) do not benefit from debate (R = H in this region); (2) deeply uncertain problems (h ≥ 3 or complex with uncertainty signals) cannot be resolved by debate (R/H ≤ 1.0). No candidate rule produces ΔAcc < 0 on Math Pro, confirming clean separability of debate-skippable questions. On College Math (R/H = 7.33), neither gate triggers — DD-lite correctly defaults to full DD when debate is overwhelmingly beneficial, demonstrating genuinely adaptive behavior.
